Tankless Water Heater vs Boiler in Middletown, DE: Which One?

A tankless water heater warms the water you use at the tap, and a boiler warms the water or steam that circulates through radiators and baseboards. People in Middletown, DE often mix the two up because both are wall-hung, both burn gas, and both sit near the same utility corner of the basement. They solve different problems, and a few units do both jobs at once.

If your hot water runs out mid-shower and your house also has hydronic heat, you may be weighing a swap that touches both systems. Before you choose, it helps to know what each machine does, what a combination boiler changes, and what the plumber measures first. A tankless water heater suits some homes very well and others poorly.

By the Middletown 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 15, 2026.

Two machines, two jobs

A tankless unit heats water only while a fixture is open. Cold water enters, a burner or electric element heats it as it passes through a heat exchanger, and the stream leaves at the set temperature, commonly around 120 degrees F. When the faucet closes, the heating stops, so there is no tank of stored water losing heat overnight.

A boiler is a closed loop. It heats water that circulates through radiators, baseboard fin tubes or in-floor tubing and then returns to be heated again. That water never reaches your faucets, and it is not the water you drink. A boiler by itself provides heat, not showers.

Some houses add an indirect tank, a small insulated cylinder that borrows heat from the boiler loop. Others install a combination boiler, which has a second heat exchanger for domestic water. Knowing which of these you already own is the first question on any visit.

Flow rate and temperature rise decide the size

Tankless units are rated by how many gallons per minute they can raise by a certain number of degrees. A shower head often uses around 2 gallons per minute, a kitchen faucet about 1.5, and a washing machine more. Add up what could run at the same time, then look at how cold the incoming water gets in winter.

If the ground-fed supply arrives at 50 degrees F and you want 120, the unit must add 70 degrees at full flow. A model that handles that at 5 gallons per minute may only manage a smaller rise when the water is colder. Undersized units show up as lukewarm showers when someone starts the dishwasher.

This is why the plumber counts fixtures and checks the incoming temperature instead of choosing by house square footage.

What gets checked before a swap

A site visit for a tankless or boiler change covers these points, in roughly this order.

  • Gas line size and length. Tankless burners draw a lot of fuel at once, and a line sized for a tank heater can be too small.
  • Venting path. Condensing units use plastic vent pipe, and the run to the exterior wall has length and elbow limits set by the maker.
  • Condensate drain. High-efficiency units create acidic condensate that needs a safe place to drain, often with a neutralizer.
  • Electrical supply. Even a gas tankless needs a nearby outlet for the fan and controls.
  • Pipe sizing and shutoffs. Isolation valves make flushing possible later, so they should be part of the install.
  • Water supply test. Mineral content affects scale, so test your tap or ask the Water Department before you pick a maintenance schedule.

Scale, flushing and what shortens a unit's life

Tankless heat exchangers have narrow passages, and mineral scale narrows them further. Many makers call for an annual flush with a descaling solution, which is easier when isolation valves are installed. A unit left alone for years can lose output or trip error codes on its own.

Tank heaters commonly need replacement around the ten-year mark, while tankless units are often described as lasting longer when maintained. A water heater repair visit is worth requesting if you see lukewarm output, rumbling, error codes or water at the unit before deciding to replace anything.

Why the swap is more than a like-for-like trade

Going from a 40-gallon tank to a wall unit moves the gas line, the vent and sometimes the water piping. That makes the labor and parts different from a tank change, even when the same fuel is used. A water heater installation on this scale starts with measurements, not a model number.

Ask for a clear price before work starts, and ask the plumber to explain anything that does not match what you already have in the basement.

Middletown, DE: gas utilities and the state plumbing code

Delaware adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code with state amendments, and those amendments address water heater piping, relief valves and expansion tanks. That means a new heater's relief valve and expansion tank are not afterthoughts. Ask the installer to point out both on the finished job.

Gas service is another place where your address matters. Townsend's resident information page lists Delmarva Power and Chesapeake Utilities as gas utilities, and which one serves a given house depends on the street, so check your own bill before calling. If you ever smell gas, leave the building, call 911, and then call your gas utility.

Questions Middletown homeowners ask

Can a tankless water heater heat my house?

No, not on its own. A tankless unit is built for domestic hot water at taps and showers. Heating radiators or baseboards takes a boiler or a combination unit built for that loop.

What is a combination boiler?

A combination boiler is one wall-hung appliance that heats the radiator loop and also heats water for the faucets on demand. It saves floor space, but when it fails both services stop. Many homeowners prefer separate systems for that reason.

Does a tankless unit need a bigger gas line?

Often it does, because the burner uses a large amount of fuel at once. The plumber measures the existing line length and size before recommending a model. Sometimes the answer is a new, larger branch off the meter.

Is a boiler the same as a hot water heater?

No. A boiler heats water for the heating system and keeps it in a closed loop. A water heater heats the water that comes out of your taps.

Which gas utility serves Townsend homes?

Townsend's resident information page lists Delmarva Power and Chesapeake Utilities as gas utilities, and the one serving you depends on your address. Your gas bill names your provider. For work in that town, see the Townsend plumbing page.

The short version

Pick the machine that matches the job: a tankless unit for taps, a boiler for radiators, a combination unit only when space or age pushes both together. The deciding facts are gas, venting and flow rate, which the plumber has to measure in your basement.
