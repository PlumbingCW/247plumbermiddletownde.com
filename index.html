What to do in the first minutes of a plumbing emergency

The first five minutes decide how much water or sewage ends up on your floors. These steps are safe for most homeowners and renters, and they work while you wait for the plumber.

  1. Get people clearMove everyone away from sagging ceilings, standing water near outlets and any sewage. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 before you do anything else.
  2. Close the main shutoffThe main valve is usually where the water line enters the house, often in a basement or utility closet near the meter. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  3. Shut off the heater and power where it is safeFor a leaking tank, close its cold supply and switch off the breaker or the gas control. Do not step into standing water to reach a panel.
  4. Stop using every drainDuring a backup, each flush or washer cycle sends another few gallons toward the same blocked line. Hold off on showers and dishes until the line is open.
  5. Photograph the damage, then callTake pictures of the source and the wet areas before you mop. A landlord or insurer will ask for them, and the plumber can read the situation faster from what you describe.

The emergencies we dispatch for in Middletown

A drain that stops everything at once, such as a tub bubbling when the washer empties, usually means a blockage in the main line. We send plumbers for clogged drains that will not clear and for hydro jetting when grease or roots keep bringing a clog back. If a camera shows the pipe itself has failed, sewer line repair is the next call.

Heat and hot water fail at the worst times. A tank that hums, rumbles or has no hot water may need a repair to the element, thermostat or valve, while a tank leaking from the bottom seam is usually finished. A leak, a sudden pressure drop or a pipe that has cracked in a freeze goes to burst pipe repair help or water line repair service on the supply side.

Smaller failures still turn into emergencies when water is running. A dripping or sprayed faucet, a disposal that hums and leaks under the sink, or a stuck shutoff valve can flood a cabinet overnight. Shops, restaurants and offices with floor drains and grease traps call for commercial plumbing service when a backup stops business.

Calls that are not emergencies and how planned work is booked

Many plumbing jobs can wait for a daytime visit. A slow drain that still empties, a faucet that drips once a minute, or a water heater that is 10 years old and working fine all fall into planned work. Planned work still goes through the phone line, and we offer same-day appointments when available.

Say what you want done when you call. A water heater installation, a switch to a tankless unit, new fixtures through plumbing installation help, or repiping a house with failing supply lines each need measurements, access and a price the plumber confirms before starting.

Some jobs raise a permit question. Middletown's Permits, Licensing and Inspections office at Town Hall, 19 W. Green Street, publishes a Plumbing Permit Application, and each phase of permitted work is inspected by the town code official. Permit rules vary, so ask the town permits office at 302-378-1171 before you assume a job does or does not need one.

Who handles which pipe: the town, Artesian and the county

The Middletown Water Department, at 431 Haveg Road, runs the town's water distribution system, the sewer collection system and the fire hydrants. Its number is 302-378-2211. If water is bubbling up in the street or a hydrant is leaking, that is the department's problem to fix, not a job for a plumber, and the town says to dial 911 for emergencies.

The town's general system pumps groundwater from the Upper Potomac and Magothy aquifers through three wells between 374 and 838 feet deep, and it also buys water from Artesian Water Company. Willow Grove Mill, Parkside and Estates at Dove Run get their water from Artesian entirely. Tell the dispatcher which system serves your street, because the utility you ask about a pressure drop depends on it.

Townsend works differently. Its resident information page lists Artesian Water for water service and New Castle County Special Services, (302) 395-5700, for sewer. A clog that starts in your house is a plumbing job. A backup that also hits your neighbors points to the main, and a call to the sewer provider is the right second call.

Delaware licensing and what licensed and insured means here

Delaware requires a Delaware license to do plumbing work, even for someone licensed in another state. The Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners sits under the Division of Professional Regulation and issues Master Plumber licenses. You can check any license yourself with the state's Verify License Online tool at delpros.delaware.gov.

Licensed means the person doing the work holds a state license for it. Insured means the contractor carries insurance for damage a job might cause. Ask the plumber to confirm that before work starts.

The state plumbing regulation, 16 Del. Admin. Code 4455, adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code with state amendments for water heater piping, relief valves and expansion tanks, so a water heater job follows those rules.

Middletown plumbing questions

Is the plumbing line really open 24/7 in Middletown?

Yes, the emergency line answers day and night, including weekends and holidays. Call and describe what is happening, and we dispatch a licensed and insured plumber to your address.

How do I request a plumber?

Call the phone line. It is the only way to request service, so there is no form to fill out and no online booking page. Have your street address, town, ZIP and a short description of the problem ready.

Do you offer same-day appointments?

Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows. Emergencies are dispatched as they come in, and planned work is booked for the first suitable opening.

When will I know what the job costs?

The plumber gives you a clear price before work starts. If you do not agree to it, the work does not begin. Ask for the price in plain terms and ask what could change it.

Which towns do you serve besides Middletown?

Dispatch covers Middletown and nearby towns, and Townsend has its own page. The service area page lists the other towns and what to tell the dispatcher about your address.

Can I check that a plumber is licensed?

Yes. Use the state's Verify License Online tool at delpros.delaware.gov and search for the name the plumber gives you. Delaware requires its own license for plumbing work, even from people licensed elsewhere.

What should I do if I smell gas?

Leave the building, call 911, then call your gas utility. Do not use switches, lighters or phones inside, and do not wait for a plumber. A plumber can work on gas piping only after the utility says the area is safe.
